The beginning

My name is Sohaib Ali! I am a Pakistani born in Ontario, Canada. When I was four years old, I was gifted my first ever gaming console: a Nintendo 3DS, which was a tiny but revolutionary handheld at the time. At least, it was for me.

Since then, my mind was always churning about the technology behind not just the Nintendo 3DS, but everything. I used to be fascinated by the integration of the top and bottom screens when I played Mario Kart DS as a kid. The two screens were so well synchronized: the position of who you were driving on the top screen was exactly the same as the one on the bottom, except the bottom screen was tracking the player on a map.

Systems thinking

The use of gyro motion when turning in other games, as well as touchscreen and microphone integration when blowing on the mic was needed, felt like a marvel of technology to me, even then. I did not realize it, but my first contact with the Nintendo 3DS sparked a lifelong passion for me.

That passion was not just about video games, but about how the internals of the technologies I used worked. This progressed even further as I grew up and became more interested in the world of computers. The internals of a computer felt so complicated but wonderful to me.

It felt like the motherboard of a computer was an entire city, with computer hardware like RAM, a GPU, and storage acting as modular pieces that made that city feel complete.

What comes next

From then on, I realized my love for technology hardware and my passion for programming from video games led me to the path of Electrical Engineering. Now, I am on the way to learning about integrated and embedded systems, the programming behind the architecture, as well as doing projects and engaging in team building that will further propel my career.

This journey is starting at the University of Michigan, where I am continuing to turn the questions I have carried for years into things I can understand, build, and share.
